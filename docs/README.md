## Core ideas in one paragraph
Dixi helps a BMW product manager turn what each market says into car profiles for the next 1, 5 or 7 Series, and keeps the reason for every decision. Inputs sit in three layers: **L1 BMW principles** (series fundamentals plus market and BMW Group principles from the team's public-source research, stored in Supabase; an AI principle assessor rates every request against them), **L2 market evidence** (customer feedback and feature requests, customer studies, sales, today's option list, plus a research agent that adds competitor features people like and market trends, each with sources) and **L3 social test** (runs only after profiles exist: which profile tracks better on social media; low weight per series: 7 Series 2 %, 5 Series 8 %, 1 Series 20 %). The PM picks a market and a series; a data script groups BMW's feedback into feature requests by category, rules set urgency, and the principle assessor adds each request's principle fit with evidence and assumptions kept apart; the PM keeps or rejects each one (a reject needs a reason), drags them into priority order and wishlists competitor or trend features. Dixi then builds three profiles that follow that priority (score = L1 principle fit + priority fit, plus L2 coverage) and saves the build as a **generation** with its profiles, decisions and priority. Every decision records who made it (name and role, from a simple local identity until there is a login) and when, and shows up in the decision log, the profile's decision trail and its PDF. With the backend connected, decisions (append-only), generations, settings and agent runs are stored in Supabase, and the next generation reads them as decision memory; without it, they stay in the browser. BMW customer text stays local and git-ignored (never sent to OpenAI or Supabase), and the public deployment runs on example data.
